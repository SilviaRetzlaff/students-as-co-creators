# Students as Co-Creators
**Making Student Work Matter Beyond the Course**


by *Silvia Retzlaff*
> How can student work become useful beyond submission and assessment?



## What happens to student work?

Think about one of your courses:

> **Which piece of student work currently disappears after submission?**

It might be ...

- a presentation
- a term paper
- a literature review
- an exercise 
- an essay
- a project report

> **Add one example to the chat.**



## From assignment to contribution

Not every assignment needs to become public or reusable.

> But there is a useful shift in perspective:
>
> **assignment for the lecturer** → **contribution for an audience**

| **Disposable assignment** | **Renewable assignment** |
|---|---|
| Created primarily for learning and assessment and usually not used again afterwards | Creates something that can have value beyond the course and can be reused, adapted or developed further |

> A disposable assignment can still be a very good assignment.  
> 
> The question is simply: **Could some student work have a second life?**




## OER as an enabling framework

**Open Educational Resources (OER)** are teaching and learning materials that are

- freely accessible,
- openly licensed,
- reusable and adaptable.

### Creative Commons
> The crucial point is **permission**.

An open licence makes it explicit what others may do with a resource.

---

For today's workshop, three licences are especially useful to recognise:

- **CC0** – broadest possible reuse (public domain)
- **CC BY** – reuse and adaptation with attribution (name the source)
- **CC BY-SA** – reuse and adaptation with attribution; adaptations remain under the same licence (name the source + use compatible licence)

### For student-created OER

> Open licensing is **not a decision lecturers can simply make on behalf of students**.

When students create original work, questions of

- authorship,
- consent,
- third-party material,
- attribution,
- and publication

need to be considered from the beginning.



## What can students create?

There is no need to start with a large OER project.

> **What is the smallest meaningful version?**

| Start small | Develop further | Larger project |
|---|---|---|
| glossary entry | explanatory text | open case archive |
| annotated reading list | H5P activity | podcast / video series |
| FAQ | case study | Wikipedia / Wikiversity contribution |
| quiz questions | learning pathway | collaborative open textbook |
| mini handout | myth + explanation | complete OER module |



### Often, the task does not need to be replaced ...
*... only redesigned:*

**presentation**  
→ reviewed explanatory resource

**literature research**  
→ annotated bibliography for future students

**practice questions**  
→ growing question bank

**case analysis**  
→ reusable case collection



### Three examples

> **1** | Improve existing knowledge

---

> **2** | Create a learning resource

---

> **3** | Build something across cohorts



#### 1. Improve existing knowledge

Students revise or extend an existing Wikipedia or Wikiversity contribution.

> **Learning:** research, source evaluation, academic writing  
> 
> **Audience:** public  
> 
> **Quality assurance:** feedback before publication



#### 2. Create a learning resource

Students create an explanatory text, interactive activity, podcast or other resource for future learners.

> **Learning:** understanding through explanation and design  
> 
> **Audience:** future students or another defined group  
> 
> **Quality assurance:** milestones, feedback and revision



#### 3. Build something across cohorts

Students create questions, cases, examples or explanations that later cohorts can use, review and improve.

> **Learning:** application, reflection and disciplinary judgement  
> 
> **Audience:** future students  
> 
> **Quality assurance:** improvement cycles throughout cohorts



## What makes co-creation work?

Before asking students to create something for others, clarify five things.

### 1. Learning
> **What should students learn by creating or adapting this resource?**

### 2. Contribution
> **What exactly will they create, improve or remix?**

### 3. Audience
> **Who could actually use it?**

### 4. Process
> **What support, feedback and quality assurance do students need before publication?**

### 5. Openness
> **Where could the resource live, and what needs to be clarified before it can be shared openly?**

Start with the learning goal, not with the publishing platform.

### Your task

Choose **one student contribution** that could become useful beyond your course.

You do **not** need to complete the entire Canvas.

For today, focus on:

1. **Student contribution**  
   What are you planning?

2. **Target group / learning situation**  
   Who should benefit from the resource?

3. **Learning goals**  
   What do your students learn by creating it?

4. **Publication / reuse**  
   For the notes-area: Where could the result live and how could it be used afterwards?

> ⏱️ **8 minutes**

> A rough idea is enough.  
> We are designing a possible next step, not a finished project.



## Let's collect some ideas

Please add one short idea to the chat:

> **My students could create / adapt ... for ...**

For example:

> My students could create short case descriptions for the next cohort.

or

> My students could improve an existing glossary for first-year students.

### Comment & pass it on

> We will look at a few ideas together.

When you comment on an idea, you might ask:

- Who would benefit from this?
- What makes it useful beyond the assignment?
- What would make it manageable?
- What would need to be clarified before sharing it?



## One thing to take away

Before we finish:

> **What is one realistic thing you could try in one of your courses?**

It does not have to be a full OER project.

---

It might simply be ...

- changing the audience of an existing assignment,
- turning an existing student product into a reusable resource,
- letting students improve something that already exists,
- or testing one small open publication.
